# Process

# Tactics

1. Identify a company with a potential
   1. target assets with long term potential
   2. with adequate numbers in the financial statements
   3. analyse the asset with AI and consider the recommendations
2. Purchase its stocks early
3. Set an auto-sell automation at certain price
4. Set a stop-loss automation for an unpredicted scenario
5. Get the profit

## Per-trade checklist (reuse every time)

- [ ] Passed fundamental check (revenue growth, FCF, debt/equity)
- [ ] Passed 3-point market check: VIX calm · RSI not overbought · price above 50/200 MA
- [ ] Entry price decided (limit order, not market)
- [ ] Stop distance set <i>first</i>, then position sized so the loss ≤ my comfort limit (respects the €1000 phase-one risk cap)
- [ ] <b>Buy</b> placed — Limit order at planned entry
- [ ] <b>Stop Loss</b> placed — downside protection
- [ ] <b>Limit sell, GTC</b> placed — auto-sell at profit target
- [ ] Added to TradingView "Holdings" list with alerts

> 📐 <b>Position sizing rule:</b> Decide your stop distance first (e.g. 15% below entry), then size the position so hitting the stop loses an amount you're comfortable with. This keeps the "risk only €1000" rule mechanical, not emotional.

# Fundamental analysis

> 💡 <b>Where to do it:</b> [StockAnalysis.com](http://stockanalysis.com/), Macrotrends, Simply Wall St, Koyfin
>
> <b>For your sectors</b> (AI, defence, nuclear): focus on <i>revenue growth</i> and <i>R&D investment</i> — these companies often have low or negative earnings today but massive future potential.

| <b>Metric</b> | <b>What it tells you</b> | <b>Good sign</b> |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <b>P/E Ratio</b> (Price/Earnings) | How expensive the stock is vs. earnings | Lower = cheaper, but context matters |
| <b>Revenue growth</b> | Is the business growing? | Consistent year-over-year growth |
| <b>EPS</b> (Earnings Per Share) | Profit per share | Rising over time |
| <b>Debt/Equity ratio</b> | How much debt the company carries | Below 1.0 is generally healthy |
| <b>Free Cash Flow</b> | Cash left after expenses — the "real" profit | Positive & growing |
| <b>Gross Margin</b> | How profitable the core business is | Higher = better moat |
| <b>ROE</b> (Return on Equity) | How efficiently the company uses shareholder money | Above 15% is strong |

# Quantitative analysis

> 💡 <b>Where to do it:</b> Finviz (free screener), Koyfin (advanced), Simply Wall St

<b>Screening</b>: Filter stocks by multiple criteria at once (e.g. "show me all defence companies with >20% revenue growth, P/E \< 30, positive free cash flow")

<b>Backtesting</b>: Testing whether a strategy would have worked historically

<b>Factor investing</b>: Looking for stocks with specific characteristics that historically outperform — value, momentum, quality, growth

| Factor | What to screen for |
| --- | --- |
| <b>Momentum</b> | Stock up 20%+ over last 6 months (winners keep winning) |
| <b>Growth</b> | Revenue/EPS growth > 15% YoY |
| <b>Quality</b> | High ROE + low debt |
| <b>Value</b> | P/E or P/S below sector average |
| <b>Earnings surprises</b> | Company beat estimates last 2–3 quarters |

# Technical analysis

> 💡 <b>Where to do it:</b> TradingView (free, all of the above built-in)

<b>Trend</b>

Price above its <b>50-day and 200-day moving average</b> = uptrend → look to buy

Price below both = downtrend → wait or avoid

<b>Support & Resistance</b>

<b>Support</b>: price level where the stock has bounced up before → good entry point

<b>Resistance</b>: price level where it has stalled before → potential exit

<b>RSI (Relative Strength Index)</b>

Tells you if a stock is oversold (\< 30) or overbought (> 70)

Buying near oversold RSI in an uptrending stock = ideal entry

<b>MACD (Moving Average Convergence Divergence)</b>

When the MACD line crosses above its signal line → momentum is turning bullish → potential buy signal

<b>Volume</b>

Big price moves on high volume = strong conviction

Price move on low volume = weak, may reverse
